Antes de começar — leia

  • As referências são ponto de partida, não roteiro fechado. Pode adaptar exercícios, trocar ângulos, mudar palavras.
  • Nos formatos com narração em off, a ordem é: 1º grava o exercício (sem áudio importa), 2º grava a explicação em vídeo separadamente.
  • No formato "exercício prático com legenda", grave o vídeo do exercício + o áudio em arquivo separado (basta colocar o celular para gravar e capturar só o áudio).
  • A legenda detalhada (séries, indicação, contraindicação) entra no texto do post, não no vídeo.

Referência · Adutores e estabilidade pélvica

Abrir no Instagram
Transcrição da referência

When it comes to a stable pelvis, everyone is obsessed with glutes, but the adductors play a huge role. Your adductors attach directly to the pelvis. That means they influence pelvic orientation, pelvic rotation, and femur position on every single rep.

If they're weak or undertrained, you lose frontal plane control. The femur may struggle into abduction and adduction depending on if they are positionally short or long, and the pelvis becomes unstable under load. And when the pelvis is unstable, the pelvic floor may responsively clench to make up for that instability, and your glutes lose mechanical advantage. So you compensate and feel less connected in your body.

Strong adductors give you better control of the femur in the socket, a more stable pelvis to produce force from, a clean foundation for the glutes to actually extend the hip, and a pelvic floor that can chill out. This is coordination, force transfer, and real strength.

If you want better movement, stronger lifts, and more efficient glutes, train the adductors like they're part of the system, and stop just doing Copenhagen planks.

Pode adaptar o tema (não precisa ser adutores) — o que importa é o formato: treino + narração técnica em off.

Referência · Cervical, postura e expansão torácica

Abrir no Instagram
Transcrição da referência

When our rib cage becomes compressed in the front, and our head starts to move forward, our lower cervical spine becomes straighter. Working on drills that expand the chest wall and help guide the scapula back can be really beneficial, but they might not stick if we don't address what this posture does at the upper cervical spine.

As our chest compresses and our lower cervical goes into flexion, our upper cervical will go into relative extension. So if I improve somebody's posture by expanding their chest or working on muscles on the back to help hold them more upright, but I don't address the upper cervical extension, when they come up they may be locked in this position of the upper cervical spine and unable to maintain this posture due to the change in their view of the horizon.

Here's a drill that works on chest expansion, scapular retraction, lower cervical extension, and upper cervical flexion. Start by sitting up next to the wall with your knees bent. Next, I'm going to take a small therapy ball and place it around the lower cervical spine. This is going to help me maintain that nice lordosis in the lower cervical spine.

I'm going to place my hands on the ground and pull my shoulder blades back slightly. And then from there, I'm going to take an inhale through my nose, imagining expanding through my chest. Exhale, let it relax. On my inhale, I want to make a small flexion or tuck down of my chin. Exhale, relax. Inhale, tuck. That upper cervical release is going to allow me to have a new horizon so that I can maintain this upright posture.

Pode adaptar o tema. O que importa é o tom: explicação técnica + demonstração prática + sensação de cuidado.

Formato 04

Problema na prática + reframe

Mostra o problema (dor crônica, postura, obesidade, depressão, sedentarismo) e apresenta o reframe Move Two Cure. Variar o tema — não é só dor.

Como gravar

1º — grave o exercício/drill prático que resolve o problema. 2º — grave a explicação em vídeo com a estrutura: problema → causa real → solução prática. A narração pode ser à câmera ou em off.

Referência · Psoas, pelve e centro de gravidade

Abrir no Instagram
Transcrição da referência (com timestamps) 00:00 — 00:15 · O problema

Do you feel like your psoas is always tight? Many people try to fix this with stretching alone, but the problem is often structural. A tight, overactive psoas usually means you're stuck in an anterior pelvic tilt or hyperlordosis. This happens because the body's center of gravity is pushed forward, forcing the psoas to work overtime to keep you upright.

00:15 — 00:40 · O reframe

The real solution isn't just stretching; it's repositioning. We need to shift that center of gravity back and allow the pelvis to rotate into a neutral position. An easy and effective drill uses only a foam roller and a wall. Lie down with your feet hip-width apart on the wall and the roller between your thighs. The key is the contact: feel your heel and the base of your big toe firm against the wall with just a light effort.

00:40 — 01:10 · A execução

By pushing gently into the wall, you'll feel the back side of your pelvis — points called the PSIS — reach the floor. This indicates your pelvis is leveling out and rotating back, which naturally helps the psoas let go. Add a deep inhale through your nose, imagining expansion through your back rib cage, and exhale gently. To progress, you can stand against the wall while maintaining that same alignment: head over rib cage, over pelvis.

01:10 — 01:30 · O fechamento

This awareness is what we call effective stacking. By shifting your weight back toward your heels and keeping this stack, you train your body to maintain an upright, functional posture without overworking the psoas. Try repeating this for about two sets of ten breaths and feel the difference in your movement.

Estrutura clara: problema → por que a solução comum falha → reframe M2C → drill prático. Pode aplicar a outros temas além de dor.
